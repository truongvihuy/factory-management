# 1. Purpose

This document provides a high-level overview of the Product Backlog for the Factory Management System (FMS).

The Product Backlog is the single prioritized list of product features, enhancements, technical capabilities, and maintenance tasks required to deliver the Factory Management System.

It serves as the foundation for Agile planning, Sprint Planning, Release Planning, and Product Roadmap management.

---

# 2. Product Vision

Develop an enterprise-grade Factory Management System capable of monitoring industrial assets in real time, managing maintenance activities, detecting equipment abnormalities, and providing operational intelligence through dashboards and reporting.

The platform shall support multiple companies, multiple factories, real-time telemetry processing, predictive maintenance, and future business expansion.

---

# 3. Product Goals

The product aims to achieve the following business objectives.

- Reduce machine downtime.
- Detect abnormal machine conditions as early as possible.
- Improve Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE).
- Reduce maintenance costs.
- Optimize energy consumption.
- Centralize factory operational data.
- Improve decision-making through analytics.
- Provide scalable architecture for future expansion.

# 6. Product Backlog Structure

The Product Backlog is organized into four hierarchical levels.

```text
Epic
    └── Feature
            └── User Story
                    └── Task
```

Example:

```text
Epic
Machine Management
    └── Feature
        Register Machine
            └── User Story
                Register a new machine
                    └── Development Tasks
```

# 8. Prioritization Method

The Product Owner prioritizes backlog items using business value and implementation urgency.

Priority definitions:

| Priority | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Critical | Required for MVP. |
| High | Strong business value; planned early after MVP. |
| Medium | Important but can be scheduled later. |
| Low | Optional enhancement or future improvement. |

---

# 9. Definition of Ready (DoR)

A User Story is considered **Ready** when:

- Business objective is clearly defined.
- Acceptance Criteria are completed.
- Dependencies are identified.
- Required UI or workflow is available (if applicable).
- Business Rules are identified.
- Estimation has been completed.
- Product Owner approves the story.

---

# 10. Definition of Done (DoD)

A User Story is considered **Done** when:

- Functional requirements are implemented.
- Acceptance Criteria are satisfied.
- Unit tests pass.
- Integration tests pass.
- Code review is completed.
- Documentation is updated.
- Audit logging is implemented (if applicable).
- Security validation is completed.
- Product Owner accepts the story.

---

# 11. Story Prioritization Criteria

Backlog priority is determined using the following factors:

- Business Value
- User Impact
- Operational Risk
- Technical Dependency
- Implementation Complexity
- Regulatory or Compliance Requirements
- Production Stability

---

# 12. Story Estimation

Story estimation follows the Fibonacci sequence.

| Story Points | Complexity |
|--------------|------------|
| 1 | Very Small |
| 2 | Small |
| 3 | Medium |
| 5 | Medium-Large |
| 8 | Large |
| 13 | Very Large |
| 21 | Epic Candidate |

Stories larger than **13 Story Points** should be split into smaller stories whenever possible.

# 14. Product Backlog Maintenance

The Product Backlog is continuously refined throughout the project.

Backlog refinement activities include:

- Adding new User Stories.
- Splitting large stories.
- Updating priorities.
- Clarifying Acceptance Criteria.
- Identifying dependencies.
- Removing obsolete backlog items.

The Product Owner is responsible for maintaining backlog priority, while the Business Analyst supports backlog refinement and requirement clarification.
